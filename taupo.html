Taupō Tours

New Zealand's largest lake: Huka Falls by jetboat, the Mine Bay rock carvings by kayak, skydives from 15,000 feet and the Tongariro Alpine Crossing an hour south.

About Taupō

Lake Taupō is the flooded caldera of one of the largest volcanic eruptions in recorded history, and the town on its north shore is the North Island's adventure hub. Huka Falls — the full Waikato River forced through a 15-metre gap — is five minutes from town and the jetboat trips run right up to the base of it. Skydiving over the lake, with Mount Ruapehu and the central plateau behind, is the other signature trip.

On the water, the Ngātoroirangi Mine Bay rock carvings are only reachable by boat; sailing yachts, launches and guided kayak tours leave from the Taupō boat harbour and take two to four hours for the round trip. The lake's rainbow trout fishery supports guided fly-fishing and trolling charters year-round.

Tūrangi, 50 minutes south at the bottom of the lake, is the base for the Tongariro Alpine Crossing shuttles and the Tongariro River rafting runs. Orakei Korako and Wairakei geothermal areas are 20 to 30 minutes north.

Best time to go: November to March

Summer brings the lake up to swimming temperature (around 20°C by February), reliable flying weather for skydives and scenic flights, and the start of the main Tongariro Alpine Crossing season — from about Labour Weekend the track is usually snow-free and the shuttle operators run their full timetable. Kayak trips to the carvings are at their calmest in the mornings; afternoon westerlies pick up on the open lake.

The Ironman in early March and school holidays from late December fill accommodation; tour availability holds up but book the small-group kayak trips a few days ahead.

What to know before you book

  • Huka Falls is 5 minutes from town; jetboat trips leave from Wairakei Tourist Park
  • The Mine Bay rock carvings are boat-access only — 2 hours by launch, 4 by kayak
  • Skydive operators meet at Taupō Airport, 10 minutes south
  • Tongariro Alpine Crossing shuttles leave from Tūrangi and Taupō before dawn; 19.4 km one way
  • Afternoon westerlies make the open lake rough — kayak tours go in the morning
  • Trout fishing needs a Taupō Fishing District licence (operators arrange it)

Taupō questions

How do you get to the Māori rock carvings at Lake Taupō?

By water only. Scenic cruises and sailing trips from the Taupō boat harbour take about 2 hours; guided kayak tours from Acacia Bay paddle there and back in 3 to 4 hours and get closest to the carvings.

Can you do the Tongariro Alpine Crossing from Taupō?

Yes. Shuttle operators pick up in Taupō and Tūrangi before dawn, drop at Mangatepopo and collect at Ketetahi. The 19.4 km crossing takes 6 to 8 hours; the main season is late October to April.

Is skydiving in Taupō good?

Taupō is the busiest drop zone in New Zealand for a reason: the view takes in the whole lake, the three central-plateau volcanoes and, on clear days, Mount Taranaki. Jumps run from 12,000 and 15,000 feet year-round, weather permitting.

What is the best way to see Huka Falls?

Walk the free viewing platform first (5 minutes from the car park), then take a jetboat if you want to be at the base of it — the Hukafalls Jet runs up the river from Wairakei every 30 minutes in summer.
