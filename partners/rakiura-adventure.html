The operation

A wooden boat and the only way across

Ulva Island sits in Paterson Inlet, is predator-free and open to the public, and holds birds that most visitors to New Zealand will not see anywhere else. There is no bridge. The ferry from Golden Bay is a ten-minute crossing and the reason the island works as a half-day trip rather than an expedition.

The same operation runs the water taxis that serve Port William, North Arm, Freshwater and the other huts on the Rakiura Great Walk and the North West Circuit — which is what turns a multi-day tramp into something you can start or finish on your own terms — plus pack transport, scenic coastal runs, sealion spotting and the historic Norwegian whaling station tour. The boat is the wooden Wairangi and reviewers mention it by name.

Before you go

Know before you go

The cheapest tier is not your price

Every fare is banded by party size and the lowest number on the booking page is the eight-or-nine-adult rate. A couple pays the 1–2 band. The prices on this page are the small-party rate for exactly that reason.

“Guaranteed departure” costs double

On the wildlife tours, booking as a single traveller with a guaranteed departure is roughly twice the per-person rate — NZ$136.78 against NZ$68.39 on the sealion trip. You are buying the boat going out regardless.

The transport reviews better than the guiding

Reviewers rate the ferry and hut transfers highly and consistently. The guided wildlife and pelagic product does not do as well: one reviewer said the skipper’s knowledge was thin on a two-hour pelagic bird tour and advised sticking to the water-taxi services. Book this operation for getting places.

Two Tripadvisor listings

“Ulva Island Ferry & Water Taxi” carries 4.9 from 97 reviews and ranks first of four transport operators. A separate “Rakiura Adventure” listing has a single three-star review. Same company.

Ninety-seven reviews is a small base.
