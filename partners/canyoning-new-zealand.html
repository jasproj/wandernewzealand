The operation

The largest range of canyons in the country

Canyoning is descending a watercourse by whatever the rock demands: abseiling the waterfalls, sliding the chutes, jumping the pools and swimming the rest. This company runs more canyons than anyone else in New Zealand, spread across the Gibbston Valley near Queenstown, Paradise at the head of Lake Wakatipu beyond Glenorchy, and Mt Aspiring National Park.

Trips are graded by how much of the day and how much commitment they ask for. Gibbston Valley takes three hours and accepts ages ten and up. Mt Aspiring runs a full day and starts at twelve. The XXL and MAX versions and the Earnslaw Burn helicopter trip are sixteen and over. Wetsuits, harnesses, helmets, socks and shoes are provided; the company describes itself as safety audited to the highest standards.

Before you go

Know before you go

Water temperature

Around seven to eight degrees. Wetsuits are provided and reviewers say they are adequate.

What is provided

Wetsuit, harness, helmet, socks and shoes. Bring swimwear and a towel.

Minimum ages

Ten for Gibbston, twelve for Paradise and standard Mt Aspiring, sixteen for XXL, MAX and the helicopter trip.

Two rates on some trips

Mt Aspiring full day and XXL each list two per-person rates on FareHarbor. Check which applies to your date before you pay.
