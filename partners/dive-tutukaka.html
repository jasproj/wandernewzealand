The operation

A reserve that has been left alone since 1998

The Poor Knights are the eroded remains of ancient volcanoes, ringed by sheer walls that drop away into clear water. The islands have been a fully protected marine reserve since 1998 and nobody is permitted to land on them, which is a large part of why the diving is what it is. The East Auckland Current pushes subtropical water down the coast, so the fish life runs well outside what the latitude would suggest.

Dive! Tutukaka has been running the trip since 1998, the same year the reserve came into full protection. It is a PADI 5-Star Instructor Development Centre and holds Qualmark Gold, New Zealand tourism’s highest sustainability grade. The day trip is built so divers and snorkellers travel on the same boat, which makes it workable for a group where not everyone dives.

Before you go

Know before you go

Departure

Tutukaka Marina, about thirty minutes from Whangārei.

Weather

The crossing is open water and trips are cancelled when the swell is up. Build a spare day into your plans.

Gear

Full hire is included on the top dive rate; bring your own and the price drops.

If you are newly certified

One reviewer with few dives logged reported a short dive because of cylinder sizing. Discuss your air consumption and preferred cylinder when you book.

Reviewers praise the organisation, the safety briefings and the crew by name, and describe the site as world class. The recurring soft spots are weather cancellations and days when swell keeps the boat in the caves rather than out at the walls; one newly qualified diver reported a short dive over cylinder sizing.

NZ$419.82 for two dives with full gear, or NZ$289.35 to come as a snorkeller. Checked October 2, 2026.

Can non-divers come?

Yes. The Perfect Day Cruise puts snorkellers on the same boat.

Why are the Poor Knights so good?

A fully protected reserve since 1998, warm subtropical water off the East Auckland Current, and no landing permitted on the islands.
