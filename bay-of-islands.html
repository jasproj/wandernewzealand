Bay of Islands Tours

144 islands, the Hole in the Rock, bottlenose dolphins, and the place New Zealand's founding treaty was signed — all from Paihia wharf.

About the Bay of Islands

The Bay of Islands is three hours north of Auckland and the warmest sailing water in the country. Paihia is the operator town — the wharf is where the Hole in the Rock cruises, dolphin trips, island-hopping ferries and sailing charters all leave — and Russell, across the water by passenger ferry, is the quieter historic village. Waitangi Treaty Grounds, a 20-minute walk from Paihia, is where the 1840 treaty between Māori and the Crown was signed.

The Hole in the Rock at Cape Brett is the standard half-day: a fast catamaran or a sailing boat out through the islands, through the rock arch if the swell allows, with dolphins on most trips. Guided kayak tours paddle the Waitangi River up to Haruru Falls, one of the few waterfalls in the country you can kayak behind, and night kayaks in summer go for the bioluminescence.

Cape Reinga and Ninety Mile Beach are a long full-day coach trip north (about 12 hours return) and are run from Paihia daily in summer.

Planning your days? Our guide to the 13 best things to do in Bay of Islands covers the walks, viewpoints, free stops and bookable trips with times and distances.

Best time to go: November to March

Northland's season starts earlier than the South Island's. By November the water is 19°C and the Hole in the Rock boats are on their full timetable; by February it is 22°C and swimming stops off the boats are routine. Bottlenose dolphins are resident and seen most of the year; common dolphins and, occasionally, orca are summer visitors. The Cape Reinga coach runs daily from November with a sand-dune boarding stop.

Waitangi Day (6 February) brings crowds and ceremonies to the Treaty Grounds; it is a good day to be there and a bad day to need a last-minute booking. Christmas to mid-January is busiest on the water.

What to know before you book

  • Paihia wharf is the departure point for almost everything; Russell is a 15-minute passenger ferry
  • Hole in the Rock cruises take 4–5 hours; passage through the arch depends on swell
  • Dolphin swimming is no longer permitted in the Bay — watching only, by DOC rule
  • Waitangi Treaty Grounds is a 20-minute walk from Paihia; allow 2–3 hours
  • Cape Reinga day trips are 12 hours return — leave before 7:30am
  • Kayak tours to Haruru Falls are tide-dependent on the Waitangi River

Bay of Islands questions

Can you swim with dolphins in the Bay of Islands?

No. The Department of Conservation ended in-water dolphin encounters in the Bay of Islands in 2019 to protect the declining bottlenose population. Cruises still watch dolphins from the boat on most trips.

How long is the Hole in the Rock cruise?

Most Hole in the Rock trips from Paihia run 4 to 5 hours, including an island stop. The boat passes through the rock arch at Cape Brett only when the swell allows; otherwise it circles close.

Is the Bay of Islands a day trip from Auckland?

It can be — about 3 hours each way plus a half-day cruise makes a 12-hour day. Two nights in Paihia is the usual recommendation, especially if you want Cape Reinga as well.

Can you kayak behind Haruru Falls?

Yes. Guided kayak tours paddle up the Waitangi River estuary and, at the right tide and river level, behind the curtain of the 5-metre horseshoe falls.
