Nelson & Abel Tasman Tours

Sea kayaks, water taxis and coastal walks into Abel Tasman National Park, plus helicopters, wineries and mountain-bike trails out of Nelson.

About Nelson and Abel Tasman

Nelson is the northern tip of the South Island and the gateway to three national parks. Abel Tasman is the one most visitors come for: a coastal track of golden beaches and estuaries with no road access past Marahau, so you get in by water taxi, by kayak, or on foot. The launch points are Marahau and Kaiteriteri, roughly an hour's drive west of Nelson past Motueka.

The classic pattern is a water taxi one way and a walk or paddle back. Water taxis run a fixed schedule up the coast and drop you at Anchorage, Bark Bay, Onetahuti or Awaroa; most people walk south for three to four hours and get collected. Guided kayak days cover the Astrolabe Roadstead and the seal colony on Tonga Island. If you have a full day, a kayak-and-walk combo does both.

Nelson itself has the Great Taste Trail cycle route through the Moutere wine country, the Nelson Lakes to the south, and helicopter operators who run Abel Tasman scenic flights and alpine landings. Golden Bay and Farewell Spit are a further two hours over the Tākaka Hill.

Planning your days? Our guide to the 14 best things to do in Nelson & Abel Tasman covers the walks, viewpoints, free stops and bookable trips with times and distances.

Best time to go: November to March

Abel Tasman is a summer park. From November the water taxis move to their full timetable, the kayak operators run morning and afternoon departures, and sea temperatures climb to the high teens by January. December 26 to mid-January is the busiest fortnight of the year; book kayaks and water taxis a week or more ahead for those dates. November and March are the quieter ends of the season with the same daylight and most of the warmth.

Tides matter here: the Awaroa estuary crossing on the Coast Track is only passable within about two hours of low tide, so check tide times before booking a one-way walk that crosses it.

What to know before you book

  • Marahau and Kaiteriteri are the launch points — about 60 minutes from Nelson by car or shuttle
  • No roads inside the park past Marahau; access is by water taxi, kayak or the Coast Track
  • Water taxis run to a schedule and drop/collect at named beaches — book the return leg
  • Awaroa estuary crossing is tide-limited (roughly ±2 hours of low tide)
  • Kayak tours are weather-dependent; operators reschedule for strong northerlies
  • Bring reef-safe sunscreen and a hat — there is little shade on the water

Nelson & Abel Tasman questions

Do I need a guide to kayak in Abel Tasman?

No. Operators hire kayaks to experienced paddlers for freedom trips, but most first-time visitors book a guided half-day or full-day because the guides read the sea conditions and handle the water-taxi logistics. Guided days usually include the Tonga Island seal colony.

How do I get from Nelson to Abel Tasman National Park?

Drive or take a shuttle west through Motueka to Marahau or Kaiteriteri, about 60 to 70 minutes. Several operators include Nelson pickup; otherwise book the shuttle separately and allow time for the first water taxi, which typically leaves around 9am.

Can you do Abel Tasman in one day from Nelson?

Yes. A water taxi up the coast at 9am, a three-to-four-hour walk back to Anchorage or Marahau, and a shuttle home is a standard day trip. Kayak-and-walk combos also fit in a day if you start early.

Is the water warm enough to swim?

In summer, yes. Sea temperatures reach 18 to 20°C from January to March. In November it is closer to 15°C — fine for a dip, cool for a long swim.
