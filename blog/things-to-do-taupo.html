14 Best Things to Do in Taupō, New Zealand

Taupō sits on the northern shore of Lake Taupō — a lake the size of Singapore in the caldera of a supervolcano — with the three volcanoes of Tongariro National Park on the southern horizon. It is the North Island's outdoor hub: the Tongariro Alpine Crossing, Huka Falls, geothermal valleys, trout fishing and the cheapest skydive in the country. This guide covers 14 things to do and how to fit them into two or three days. Planning the trip? Start with our Taupō tours for live prices from local operators.

The lake

1. Māori rock carvings at Mine Bay by boat

Cruise 90 min–2.5 hrs

The 14-metre carving of Ngātoroirangi, the tohunga who guided the Te Arawa canoe, was cut into the cliff at Mine Bay in the late 1970s by Matahi Whakataka-Brightwell and is only reachable by water. Sailing catamarans, the 1960s-vintage Ernest Kemp and faster launches all run from the Taupō marina; the sailing trips are the quiet option, the evening cruise adds sunset over the western bays.

2. Kayak to the rock carvings

Kayak Half day

Guided kayak trips leave from Acacia Bay for the 7-km round trip to Mine Bay, with the carvings viewed from water level, where they were meant to be seen. Mornings are calmer; the lake can chop up fast in the afternoon. Expect 3–4 hours including the briefing.

3. Swim at the Taupō lakefront and hot-water beach

Free Summer

The lakefront by the town centre has a swimming beach and a hot-water stream at Otumuheke, where the Waikato River leaves the lake — the Spa Park hot pools are free and sit where the hot stream meets the river. Water temperature in the lake reaches about 20°C in February.

4. Floatplane over the lake and volcanoes

Scenic flight 20–60 min

Floatplanes take off from the lake beside the marina for flights over Huka Falls and the Wairakei steamfield, out to the Orakei Korako geothermal valley (where you land and walk the terraces), or south over the Tongariro volcanoes. Thirty minutes is enough for the lake and falls; the Orakei Korako landing is the standout.

Rivers, falls and geothermal

5. Huka Falls

Free 30 min

The Waikato River narrows from 100 metres to 15 and drops 11 metres at Huka Falls, pushing about 220,000 litres a second through the gap. The viewing bridge is a five-minute walk from the car park, 5 km north of town; the Huka Falls Walkway from Spa Park (1 hr one way) follows the river to the falls and is the better approach. Jet boats run up to the base from below.

6. Orakei Korako geothermal valley

Geothermal 2 hrs

The 'Hidden Valley' is reached by a short boat crossing of Lake Ohakuri, 25 minutes north of Taupō, and holds silica terraces, geysers and Ruatapu Cave with its warm pool — with a fraction of Rotorua's crowds. Allow two hours on the boardwalks. Combine with the floatplane for the full version.

7. Craters of the Moon

Geothermal 45 min

A steaming thermal field 5 km north of town, with a boardwalk loop through hissing fumaroles and bubbling mud, created when the Wairakei power station lowered the water table in the 1950s. Cheap entry, no queues, and best on a cool morning when the steam shows.

8. Wairakei Terraces hot pools

Hot pools Evening

Adults-only silica-blue thermal pools of varying temperatures, fed from the Wairakei geothermal field, with man-made terraces modelled on the lost Pink and White Terraces. Open late, 10 minutes from town. The DeBretts pools closer to town take families.

Tongariro and adventure

9. Tongariro Alpine Crossing

Hike 6–8 hrs

The 19.4-km one-way crossing between the Mangatepopo and Ketetahi road ends passes the Red Crater (1,886 m) and the Emerald Lakes, and is the best day walk in the country. Since 2023 you need a free DOC booking from October to April. It is a 1-hour drive from Taupō to the trailheads; shuttle operators park you at the finish and drop you at the start so the car is waiting. Weather on the ridge turns fast — check the DOC forecast the night before.

10. Skydive over Lake Taupō

Adrenaline 12,000–18,500 ft

Taupō airport runs the busiest tandem skydiving operation in the country, with the lake, the volcanoes and on clear days both coasts visible from altitude. Jumps go from 12,000 to 18,500 feet. Prices are the lowest in New Zealand for the height, which is why it is here.

11. Trout fishing on the Tongariro River

Fishing Guided

The Tongariro at Tūrangi, 50 minutes south, is one of the best wild rainbow and brown trout rivers anywhere, with the winter spawning run (May–September) the famous season and summer dry-fly fishing on the upper pools. A Taupō fishing licence is separate from the national one; guides supply it with the gear.

12. Bungy and swing at the Waikato River

Adrenaline 47 m

The Taupō bungy platform sits 47 metres above the Waikato River gorge north of town, with a water-touch option and a cliffhanger swing on the same platform. The viewing deck is free if you are the one holding the bag.

13. Huka Prawn Park and the Honey Hive

Family 2 hrs

On the river below Huka Falls, a prawn farm heated by geothermal water where you can fish for your lunch, plus a water-play area for kids. The Honey Hive next to Craters of the Moon does mānuka tastings and a glass hive. Both are 10 minutes from town and good with children.

14. Mountain bike the Great Lake Trail

MTB Half–full day

A 71-km grade-3 trail along the western shore of the lake in sections — W2K (Whakaipo Bay to Kinloch, 14 km) is the day ride, with a water-taxi return across the bay. Bike hire and shuttles run from town. Spring and autumn are the best riding conditions; summer afternoons get hot and exposed.

Best time to go: November to March

November to March. The Tongariro Alpine Crossing is a summer walk — snow and ice on the Red Crater make it an alpine route from May to October — and the DOC booking system runs through the same months. The lake reaches about 20°C in February, the rock-carving cruises run their full timetable, and Huka Falls is at its loudest after spring rain.

Taupō questions

How many days do you need in Taupō?

Two to three. One for the Tongariro Alpine Crossing (a full day with the drive), one for the lake and rock carvings, and a half day for Huka Falls, Craters of the Moon and the hot pools.

Do you need to book the Tongariro Alpine Crossing?

Yes, from October to April a free DOC booking is required. Shuttles from Taupō and Tūrangi should be booked separately, a day or more ahead in summer. The crossing is one-way, so a shuttle or a car shuffle is essential.

How do you get to the Māori rock carvings in Taupō?

By water only — sailing catamarans, the Ernest Kemp cruise boat and guided kayak trips all go from Taupō; the kayak trips leave from Acacia Bay. Allow 90 minutes to 4 hours depending on the option.

What is the best time to visit Taupō?

November to March for the lake, the crossing and long days; the lake is warmest in February. The Tongariro Alpine Crossing is safest in summer; winter crossings need alpine gear and a guide.
