18 Best Things to Do in Wellington, New Zealand

Wellington is the capital most visitors skip — a stop between the North Island and the South Island ferry. That's a mistake. It's the most walkable city in New Zealand, has the best coffee and craft beer in the country, a free national museum, a fenced wildlife sanctuary ten minutes from downtown, and the film studios that built Middle-earth. It's also relentlessly windy. Here's what to do with two or three days.

Museums & Culture

1. Te Papa Tongarewa

National museum NZ$35 international / free NZ residents Half day

New Zealand's national museum, on the waterfront. Six floors covering Māori taonga (treasures), a colossal squid, an earthquake simulator, and the Gallipoli exhibition — a WWI story told through 2.4-times-life-size sculptures built by Weta Workshop that is the most affecting museum display in the country. International visitors pay NZ$35 (under-16s free); locals go free. Allow three hours minimum. Open daily 10am–6pm.

2. Weta Workshop Unleashed

Miramar From NZ$55 90 min–3 hrs

The special effects studio behind The Lord of the Rings, Avatar, and District 9, in the Miramar suburb 20 minutes from the city. Weta Workshop Unleashed is the main experience — a guided walk through three fictional film productions showing how props, prosthetics, and creatures are made. The Workshop Tour goes behind the scenes at the actual studio. Book the combo if you're a film person. Bus 2 from downtown.

3. Wellington Museum

Free Waterfront 1 hour

In an 1892 bond store on Queens Wharf. Smaller and quieter than Te Papa, focused on the city's maritime and social history — the Wahine ferry disaster of 1968, Māori legends of the harbor, and a top floor that's a genuine curiosity cabinet. Free, and a good rainy-hour option next to the waterfront.

Views & Outdoors

4. Wellington Cable Car

NZ$11 return Every 10 min Lambton Quay

A five-minute ride from Lambton Quay in the CBD up to Kelburn, 120 metres above the harbor. Running since 1902. The view from the top over the city and harbor is the classic Wellington postcard. Most people ride up and walk down through the Botanic Garden. There's a small free Cable Car Museum at the top and the Space Place observatory next door.

5. Wellington Botanic Garden

Free Kelburn 1–2 hours

25 hectares on the hillside below the Cable Car terminus — native forest, a rose garden with 3,000 plants, a begonia house, and paths that wind down to Thorndon and the Bolton Street cemetery. Walking down from the Cable Car through the garden and back into town takes about an hour and is the best free thing to do in Wellington.

6. Mount Victoria Lookout

Free 196m 45-min walk or drive

The 360-degree view: harbor, city, airport, Cook Strait, and on a clear day the Kaikōura Ranges on the South Island. Walk up from Oriental Bay or Courtenay Place in about 45 minutes through the town belt — or drive, or take bus 20. Lord of the Rings fans: the "Get off the road!" scene where the hobbits hide from the Nazgûl was filmed in the trees on the way up. Go at sunset.

7. Oriental Bay

Free City beach 15-min walk from Te Papa

Wellington's city beach — a golden crescent (the sand is imported from Golden Bay) with a swimming raft, a fountain, Norfolk pines, and art deco apartments behind. Safe swimming in summer with lifeguards. The walk from Te Papa along the waterfront to Oriental Bay and on to the Freyberg Pool is the standard evening promenade.

8. Red Rocks Seal Colony

Free May–October for seals 2–4 hrs return

A rugged south-coast walk from Owhiro Bay along a 4WD track to red volcanic rocks where New Zealand fur seals haul out in winter (May to October — up to 300 of them). The full walk to Sinclair Head is 7 km return; the seals start about 3 km in. Exposed and windy; bring layers. Bus 29 to Owhiro Bay.

Local tip: Wellington's weather changes by the hour. If the morning is clear, do Mount Victoria or the Cable Car first — cloud and wind usually build through the afternoon. Save Te Papa and Weta for when it turns.

Wildlife

9. Zealandia Te Māra a Tāne

NZ$26 adult Karori 2–3 hrs · Night tours NZ$95

The world's first fully fenced urban eco-sanctuary — 225 hectares of regenerating forest behind a predator-proof fence, ten minutes from downtown. Eighteen native species have been reintroduced, including takahē (there are only about 500 left), kākā parrots, tuatara, and kiwi. You'll see and hear more native birds here in an hour than in a week elsewhere. The two-hour guided night tour is the best chance to see a wild kiwi in New Zealand. Free shuttle from the Cable Car top station.

10. Matiu / Somes Island

Ferry NZ$30 return Harbor Half day

A predator-free island in the middle of Wellington Harbour, 20 minutes by East by West ferry from Queens Wharf. Former quarantine station and WWII gun emplacements, now a DOC reserve with tuatara, weta, little blue penguins, and a summit walk with views back to the city. Bring lunch — there's nothing on the island. Bags are checked for rodents on arrival.

Cuba Street & the Neighborhoods

11. Cuba Street

Free Te Aro Any time

Wellington's bohemian spine — a pedestrianized strip of vintage shops, record stores, street performers, Fidel's cafe, and the Bucket Fountain (a kinetic sculpture that's been splashing passers-by since 1969). The side streets hold the city's best small restaurants and bars. It's the neighborhood that gives Wellington its reputation as the creative capital.

12. Wellington Waterfront

Free Queens Wharf to Oriental Bay

A 2-km promenade past Te Papa, the Wharewaka (Māori canoe house), the bronze Solace in the Wind statue leaning into the gale, and a jumping platform where locals leap into the harbor. Sunday mornings the Harbourside Market sets up beside Te Papa with produce and street food. On a calm day it's the best walk in the city.

13. Hannahs Laneway

Off Cuba St Food

A short alley off Leeds Street that packs in Wellington Chocolate Factory (tours and tastings), Fix & Fogg peanut butter (window service), Leeds Street Bakery, Shepherd restaurant, and Golding's Free Dive bar. Everything is small-batch and made on site. Good for a grazing lunch.

Coffee & Craft Beer

14. The Coffee Scene

NZ$5–6 a flat white More cafes per capita than New York

Wellington claims to have invented the flat white (Auckland and Sydney dispute it) and takes coffee seriously. Havana Coffee Works on Tory Street roasts on site. Customs by Coffee Supreme on Ghuznee Street is the purist's choice. Midnight Espresso on Cuba Street is the late-night institution. Flight Coffee Hangar is the specialty option. Prefab on Jessie Street does the best brunch.

15. The Craft Beer Trail

20+ breweries Te Aro Evening

Wellington is the craft beer capital of New Zealand. Garage Project's cellar door on Aro Street is the essential stop — they release new beers weekly. Parrotdog in Lyall Bay, Fortune Favours on Leeds Street, and Heyday on Cuba Street round out the walkable core. Hashigo Zake is the underground bar with the deepest tap list. Beervana, the national beer festival, is in August.

Day Trips

16. Martinborough Wine Region

1 hr 15 min drive Pinot Noir Tours from NZ$160

Over the Remutaka Hill in the Wairarapa, Martinborough is a small grid-plan village with 20-odd boutique wineries within cycling distance of the square. It's New Zealand's best Pinot Noir outside Central Otago. Rent a bike in the village and ride between Ata Rangi, Palliser, and Martinborough Vineyard, or book a tour from Wellington. Train to Featherston plus a shuttle works without a car.

17. Cape Palliser

2 hr 30 min drive Seals, lighthouse Full day

The southernmost point of the North Island, past Martinborough on a wild coast road. A fur seal colony (the biggest on the North Island) sprawls over the rocks by the road, and a 250-step climb reaches the lighthouse with views across Cook Strait to the Kaikōura mountains. The Putangirua Pinnacles on the way — eroded rock spires — were the Paths of the Dead in The Return of the King. Car needed.

18. Kāpiti Island

1 hr drive + ferry From NZ$95 Permit required

A predator-free nature reserve island off the Kāpiti Coast north of Wellington, and one of the best places in the country to see rare birds — kōkako, saddleback, takahē, and kiwi on overnight stays. Numbers are capped at 160 visitors a day; book the ferry from Paraparaumu well ahead. The summit track climbs 521 metres for views up and down the coast.

Practical Tips

Getting there. Wellington Airport is 20 minutes from the city by bus (Airport Express) or taxi. Direct flights from Auckland (1 hour), Christchurch, Queenstown, and Australia. The Interislander and Bluebridge ferries to Picton on the South Island take about 3.5 hours and leave from the waterfront — book cars weeks ahead in summer.

Getting around. Walk. The center is compact and flat along the waterfront, steep up the hills. Metlink buses cover everything else; a Snapper card is cheaper than cash. The Cable Car and Zealandia shuttle connect at the top. You don't need a car unless you're heading to the Wairarapa.

Weather. Windy. Wellington averages over 170 days a year with gusts above 60 km/h. Summer (December–February) is 17–22°C and the calmest. Winter (June–August) is 8–12°C, wet, and wild. Pack a windproof layer in every season and forget umbrellas.

Budget. Wellington is cheaper than Auckland and Queenstown. Hostels NZ$40–60, mid-range hotels NZ$180–280, top-end NZ$350+. Coffee NZ$5–6, a pint NZ$12–14, a restaurant main NZ$30–40. Many of the best things — Te Papa (for residents), the Botanic Garden, Mount Victoria, Cuba Street, the waterfront — are free.

Local tip: If you're taking the ferry south, spend the night before in Wellington rather than arriving the same day — Cook Strait crossings get cancelled in bad weather and rebooking from the ferry terminal is miserable. Early morning sailings are the calmest.
