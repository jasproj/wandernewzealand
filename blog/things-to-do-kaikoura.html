13 Best Things to Do in Kaikōura, New Zealand

Kaikōura is a small town on a peninsula where the seabed drops to over a kilometre within sight of the beach — which is why sperm whales, dusky dolphins and fur seals are here year-round, and why the town's name means 'eat crayfish'. The 2016 earthquake lifted the coast by up to six metres and rebuilt the road; the wildlife never left. This guide covers 13 things to do and when each works best. Planning the trip? Marine wildlife

1. Whale watching by boat

Whales 3 hrs

Resident male sperm whales feed in the Kaikōura Canyon all year and the boat tours find them with hydrophones, usually within 30–45 minutes of leaving South Bay. Humpbacks pass through in June–July and orca appear in summer. Trips run from early morning and cancel in rough seas, so plan for a spare half day. Motion-sickness tablets are not optional for most people.

2. Whale watching by plane or helicopter

Scenic flight 30–50 min

Fixed-wing flights from the Kaikōura airfield show the whole shape of the whale from above — the tail, the blow, the dive — and cover more water than the boats in half the time, with no seasickness. Helicopter tours do the same at lower altitude. Both depend on cloud base and go when the boats are grounded by swell.

3. Swim with dusky dolphins

Wildlife 3 hrs

Pods of several hundred dusky dolphins live off the peninsula and the swim tours put you in the water with them, in supplied wetsuits, from South Bay. The dolphins are fast and curious; the operator briefs you on making noise through your snorkel to hold their interest. Numbers in the water are capped, so book days ahead in summer. Dawn departures have the calmest sea.

4. Kayak with the fur seals

Kayak 2.5–3 hrs

Guided sea-kayak trips paddle the peninsula's coast to the seal colony at the Point, with the Seaward Kaikōura Range as the backdrop and dolphins often alongside. Calm, sheltered water on the lee side; no experience needed. The sunset departure is the quiet one.

5. Walk to the seal colony at Point Kean

Free 30 min

At the end of the peninsula road, fur seals haul out on the rock platform that the 2016 quake lifted out of the water. Walk the platform at low tide only and keep 10 metres from the seals — they bite. The Ōhau Point colony 27 km north has a pup nursery pool visible from the road in spring.

On land

6. Kaikōura Peninsula Walkway

Walk 3 hrs loop

An 11.7-km loop from town over the cliff tops to Point Kean and back along South Bay, with seals below, seabirds on the stacks and the mountains inland. Three hours at a walking pace; the cliff-top section is the one not to skip. The shorter Point Kean to South Bay section is 1 hour.

7. Llama trek the peninsula

Family 1–2.5 hrs

Walk the peninsula coast or the hills behind town leading a llama on a halter — a Kaikōura oddity that has been running for years and works especially well with children. Taster treks are an hour; the half-day version climbs for the view over the bay.

8. Mount Fyffe

Hike 6–8 hrs return

The 1,602-metre summit of Mount Fyffe stands directly behind the town and the track (a 4WD road to the hut, then a ridge) gives the view that explains Kaikōura — the whole coast, the canyon water colour, and the Seaward Kaikōura Range behind. Eight hours return from the car park; the hut at 1,100 m splits it into an overnight.

9. Fishing and crayfishing charter

Fishing 2–4 hrs

Charter boats run short trips from South Bay for blue cod, perch and sea perch, and check craypots on the way — a fresh crayfish for lunch is the Kaikōura outcome. Two-hour trips suit families; four hours gets further out. Morning sea conditions are usually better.

10. Eat crayfish at Nin's Bin

Food Roadside

The caravan at Rakautara, 20 km north of town on the coast road, has sold cooked crayfish from the same spot since 1977. Half a cray with garlic butter eaten on the rocks across the road is the Kaikōura meal. Cash and card; it sells out by mid-afternoon in summer. Kaikōura Seafood BBQ at Point Kean is the in-town alternative.

11. Hot tub or sauna at the lagoon

Relax Evening

Wood-fired hot tubs and a sauna by the lagoon at the holiday park, booked by the session, with the mountains behind. The right end to a day on the water in the shoulder months when the sea has been cold.

12. Dive the peninsula

Scuba Half day

Kaikōura's kelp forests, seal colonies and the canyon drop-off give shore and boat dives with seals, crayfish and blue cod in close. Try-dives for beginners run from the shore; certified divers get the boat sites. Visibility is best in autumn and winter when the rivers run low.

13. Fyffe House

Heritage 30 min

The oldest building in Kaikōura, an 1840s whaler's cottage built on whale-bone piles on the peninsula road, run by Heritage New Zealand. Small, pink, and a 30-minute window into what the town was before the tour boats — the whaling station that gave the peninsula its first European settlement.

Best time to go: November to March

November to March brings the calmest seas and the fewest cancelled boat trips, orca in the bay, seal pups at Ōhau Point from December, and swimmable water for the dolphin tours. Sperm whales are here every month. Expect 18–24°C on shore, cooler on the water; the sea breeze builds most afternoons, so book the morning departures.

Kaikōura questions

What is the best month for whale watching in Kaikōura?

Sperm whales are resident year-round, so any month works. June and July add migrating humpbacks; summer (December–March) adds orca and calmer seas with fewer cancellations. See our full guide to the best time of year for whale watching.

How many days do you need in Kaikōura?

Two nights. The whale and dolphin trips cancel in rough seas, so a spare morning is insurance. Day one: whales and the Peninsula Walkway; day two: dolphin swim or seal kayak, crayfish, and the coast road.

Can you see seals in Kaikōura for free?

Yes — the Point Kean colony at the end of the peninsula road and the Ōhau Point colony 27 km north are both roadside. Stay 10 metres back and off the rock platform at high tide.

How far is Kaikōura from Christchurch?

180 km, about 2 hours 30 minutes north on State Highway 1, on the coast road rebuilt after the 2016 earthquake. The Coastal Pacific train runs the same route in summer.
