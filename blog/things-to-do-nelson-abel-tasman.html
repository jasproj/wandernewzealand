14 Best Things to Do in Nelson & Abel Tasman, New Zealand

Nelson sits at the top of the South Island with the sunniest weather in the country and three national parks within an hour's drive. Abel Tasman is the reason most people come: a 60-km coast of golden beaches and granite headlands that you reach by water taxi, kayak or on foot. This guide covers 14 things to do across the city, the park and Golden Bay, with the practical details — which bay to aim for, how the tides work, what to book ahead in summer. Planning the trip? Abel Tasman National Park

1. Kayak the Abel Tasman coast from Marahau or Kaiteriteri

Kayak Half–full day

Sea kayaking is the way to see the park. Guided half-day paddles cover Marahau to Apple Tree Bay or Adele Island, where fur seals haul out on the rocks; full-day and multi-day trips reach Anchorage, Bark Bay and Tonga Island Marine Reserve. Mornings are calmer before the afternoon sea breeze. Independent hire is possible with a briefing if you have paddled before.

2. Walk a day section of the Abel Tasman Coast Track

Great Walk 3–5 hrs

The full Coast Track is 60 km over 3–5 days, but a water taxi lets you walk one section in a day. The classic is Anchorage to Marahau (12.4 km, 4 hours) or Bark Bay to Anchorage (11.5 km) via the Falls River swing bridge. Tides matter at Torrent Bay and Awaroa — the estuary crossings only open within about two hours of low tide.

3. Water taxi to Anchorage, Bark Bay or Awaroa

Boat From Kaiteriteri

Water taxis leave Kaiteriteri and Marahau from about 8.30 am and run a scheduled loop up the coast, dropping walkers and kayakers at the main bays and collecting them in the afternoon. Even if you do not walk, the trip itself is a scenic cruise past Split Apple Rock and the seal colony. Book the day before in January.

4. Skydive over Abel Tasman

Adrenaline Motueka

Tandem jumps from Motueka airfield, 10 minutes from the park, with the coast, Golden Bay and the Kahurangi ranges under you. Jumps run from 10,000 ft up to 18,000 ft, which is the highest commercial tandem in the country. Morning slots have the stillest air and the clearest views across to the North Island.

5. Split Apple Rock

Photo Kaiteriteri

A granite boulder sitting in the shallows between Kaiteriteri and Marahau, split cleanly down the middle. It is a 20-minute walk from the car park on Tokongawa Drive at low tide, or most water taxis and kayak trips pass it. Go in the morning when the sun is behind you.

6. Swim at Kaiteriteri Beach

Beach Free

The golden crescent at the park's southern entrance is the region's swimming beach — sheltered, shallow and warm by South Island standards from December to March. The Kaiteriteri Mountain Bike Park sits in the hills behind it with a beginner-friendly network, and there is paddleboard and kayak hire on the sand.

Nelson city

7. Nelson Saturday Market

Market 8am–1pm

Montgomery Square fills every Saturday with around 100 stalls — produce from the Moutere and Motueka orchards, local cheese, craft, and breakfast from a dozen food trucks. It has run since 1981 and is the best single morning in the city. The Sunday Monty's Market is smaller and more about secondhand goods.

8. Mountain bike Codgers or the Coppermine

MTB All grades

Nelson's Codgers Mountain Bike Park starts 10 minutes' ride from the centre with grade 2–5 trails up the Grampians and Fringed Hill, and the Dun Mountain / Coppermine loop is a 43-km backcountry ride on a historic railway bench. Shuttle operators run uplifts for gravity days; full-suspension and e-bike hire is available in town.

9. Centre of New Zealand walk

Free 1 hr return

A short, steep climb from the Botanical Reserve at the end of Hardy Street to a survey marker on a 147-metre hill, with the city, Tasman Bay and the Western Ranges laid out below. Thirty minutes up at a steady pace. Go late afternoon for the light on the bay.

10. Nelson's craft beer and wine

Food & drink Half day

Nelson grows most of the country's hops, so the brewery scene is real: McCashin's, Sprig & Fern, Eddyline and Hop Federation (in Riwaka) all have taprooms. The Moutere Hills and Upper Moutere wineries — Neudorf, Seifried — are 30 minutes out and better known for chardonnay and aromatics than Marlborough-style sauvignon.

11. World of WearableArt and Classic Cars Museum

Rainy day 2 hrs

The WOW museum on Quarantine Road shows past winners of the World of WearableArt competition, garments built from wood, light, metal and found objects, beside a hall of 140 classic cars. Not what you expect in a small city and a good wet-weather hour. Nelson gets around 2,400 sunshine hours a year, so you may not need it.

Golden Bay and beyond

12. Drive over Tākaka Hill to Golden Bay

Day trip Scenic

The Tākaka Hill road climbs 791 metres over a marble karst range with lookouts back across Tasman Bay, then drops into Golden Bay. Stop at Hawkes Lookout (15 minutes return) on the summit. It is 1 hour 15 minutes from Motueka to Tākaka; allow a full day to reach Wharariki or Farewell Spit and return.

13. Te Waikoropupū Springs

Free 30 min

Just outside Tākaka, Pupu Springs discharge around 14,000 litres a second of some of the clearest fresh water ever measured — visibility of 63 metres. A 30-minute boardwalk loop circles the main pool. The springs are tapu to Ngāti Tama and Ngāti Rārua; no contact with the water is allowed.

14. Wharariki Beach and Farewell Spit

Beach Golden Bay

At the top of the South Island, a 20-minute walk over farmland reaches Wharariki — dunes, the Archway Islands and fur seal pups in the rock pools at low tide in summer. Farewell Spit, the 26-km sand spit beside it, is a bird sanctuary; public access ends at 4 km, and licensed 4WD tours run to the lighthouse.

Best time to go: November to March

November to March is the window. The sea is swimmable from December, the kayak season runs at full schedule, and Golden Bay roads are clear. Nelson averages more sunshine hours than anywhere else in New Zealand, so rainy-day backups are rarely needed. Tide tables matter on the Coast Track; check them before you set a date.

Nelson & Abel Tasman questions

How many days do you need for Nelson and Abel Tasman?

Three days minimum: one in Abel Tasman (kayak or walk a section with a water taxi), one in Nelson city and the wineries, one for Golden Bay. The full Coast Track adds three to five days.

Do you need to book Abel Tasman water taxis and kayaks in advance?

In December and January, yes — a day or two ahead for water taxis and guided kayak trips. Outside peak season you can usually book the day before. Independent kayak hire requires a safety briefing and good weather.

Can you walk part of the Abel Tasman Coast Track in one day?

Yes. Take a water taxi to Anchorage or Bark Bay in the morning and walk back toward Marahau, or walk north and get collected. Anchorage to Marahau is 12.4 km, about 4 hours, on an easy graded track.

What is the best time to visit Abel Tasman?

November to March for warm water, long days and the most reliable weather; Nelson is the sunniest region in the country. December–January is busiest. Late November and March have the best balance of weather and space.
