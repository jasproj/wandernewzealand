The Wanaka Tree: New Zealand's Most Photographed Willow

A few metres off the Wānaka lakefront, in the shallows of one of Central Otago's most beautiful glacial lakes, stands a lone willow tree. It grows directly in the water, its roots in the lake bed, with the Southern Alps arranged behind it across the water. There is nothing structurally remarkable about it — it's a willow, common in New Zealand — but its position, its solitude, and the backdrop it sits against have made it arguably the most-reproduced natural image in the country. Understanding why requires spending some time on the shore.

Why It Works

The composition

Lake Wānaka, Central Otago South Island

Lake Wānaka is around 43km long and sits at 278m above sea level in a glacially carved valley. The Buchanan Peaks and other ranges of Mount Aspiring National Park rise steeply on the western and southern shores. The tree grows in the lake a few metres from the north shore path near the town centre, facing east, with the mountains arranged across the water directly behind it.

On a calm morning the lake surface acts as a mirror. The tree's reflection extends to double its height. The mountains behind double as well. The effect — a single organic form in a vast aquatic mirror, mountains framing it on three sides — is why the image works. It is compositionally simple and hard to take badly, which is partly why it has been photographed millions of times.

Through the seasons

Year-round access

The tree changes character through the year. In winter it is bare, the stripped branches against snow-capped peaks and grey water — stark and architectural. In spring it comes into the tentative green of new leaves. Summer brings full deep green, the surrounding mountains visible against blue sky. Autumn in Central Otago is spectacular: the surrounding poplars and willows turn gold and orange, and the Wānaka tree follows suit. Most landscape photographers prefer winter or autumn for the drama; most casual visitors arrive in summer.

Lake Wānaka beyond the tree

43 km long Mount Aspiring NP access

The lake is one of the clearest and most beautiful in the South Island. Kayaking on Wānaka gives access to the southern and western shores that are inaccessible by road, with views back to the mountains and the town. Roy's Peak — the most popular day hike in the Wānaka area — climbs from just above the town to 1,578m, with a ridge walk offering panoramic views of the lake and the surrounding ranges. The trailhead is about 6km from the town centre.

Getting there: Wānaka is about 45 minutes from Queenstown via the Crown Range (steep but spectacular) or about an hour via Cromwell on State Highway 6. The tree is on the lakefront path, a 10-minute walk from the town centre. There is no admission fee. Come early on a still morning for the reflection — wind by 9am most days breaks the mirror effect.
