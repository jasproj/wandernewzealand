13 Best Things to Do in Bay of Islands, New Zealand

The Bay of Islands is 144 islands in a sheltered subtropical bay three hours north of Auckland — the warmest sailing water in the country, the site of New Zealand's founding document, and the base for the long day north to Cape Reinga. Paihia is the hub, Russell across the water is the quieter one, Kerikeri has the history and the oranges. This guide covers 13 things to do, with the ones that need booking in summer flagged. On the water

1. Cruise to the Hole in the Rock

Boat 4 hrs

Motukōkako, the island at the mouth of the bay with a 16-metre sea-cave passage through it, is the trip everyone takes — launches from Paihia and Russell thread the boat through the hole in calm seas, stop at Urupukapuka Island and run with the dolphins when they find them. Half a day. Our Hole in the Rock guide covers the options.

2. Sail the bay on a classic yacht

Sailing Full day

Day sails leave Russell and Paihia on gaff-rigged schooners and racing yachts, anchoring in a bay for a swim and a snorkel and letting passengers take the helm. It is slower than the launches and the better way to understand why the bay became a sailing destination. Lunch is usually included; book two days ahead in January.

3. Kayak to Haruru Falls

Kayak 2–3 hrs

Guided paddles follow the tidal Waitangi River through mangroves from the bay up to Haruru Falls, a horseshoe fall you can paddle right under at the right tide. Night tours do the same route under the stars with bioluminescence in the water on dark nights. Easy paddling; the cruise-and-kayak version takes a boat up and paddles back.

4. Swim with or watch the dolphins

Wildlife Half day

Bottlenose dolphins live in the bay year-round. Swimming with them has been restricted since 2019 to protect the resident pod, so most trips are now watch-only, and the Hole in the Rock cruises spend time with any pod they meet. Dedicated wildlife cruises add fur seals and, in winter, the odd passing whale.

5. Snorkel and beach-hop at Urupukapuka Island

Island Day

The largest island in the bay has walking tracks across its headlands, several swimming beaches and a DOC campground, 30 minutes by ferry from Paihia. The Otehei Bay ferry runs several times a day in summer; go early to have the beaches before the cruise boats stop at midday.

History and towns

6. Waitangi Treaty Grounds

History 2–3 hrs

Where the Treaty of Waitangi was signed in 1840, with the Treaty House, the carved meeting house Te Whare Rūnanga, the 35-metre waka Ngātokimatawhaorua, and two museums that give the clearest account of the treaty and what followed. A guided tour and the cultural performance are included; allow three hours. Ten minutes' walk from Paihia.

7. Take the ferry to Russell

Town Half day

Russell was Kororāreka, the first European settlement and once the 'hellhole of the Pacific'; now it is a quiet waterfront village with Christ Church (1836, musket holes still in the weatherboards), Pompallier Mission's working printery and the Duke of Marlborough Hotel, holder of New Zealand's first liquor licence. Fifteen minutes by passenger ferry from Paihia; the Flagstaff Hill walk takes 30 minutes.

8. Kerikeri: Stone Store and Kemp House

Heritage Half day

Twenty minutes inland, the Stone Store (1836) and Kemp House (1822) at the Kerikeri Basin are the oldest stone and wooden buildings in the country, with the Rewa's Village reconstructed pā across the river and Rainbow Falls a 20-minute walk upstream. Kerikeri's Sunday market and the orchard shops are the food stop. Electric boat hire runs from the Stone Store on the inlet.

9. Kawiti glowworm caves

Caves 45 min

At Waiomio near Kawakawa, 25 minutes from Paihia, the Kawiti family has guided visitors through this limestone cave on their land since the 1950s — a 200-metre walk under glowworm ceilings with far fewer people than Waitomo. Combine with Haruru Falls and the Hundertwasser toilets in Kawakawa on the way.

Day trips

10. Cape Reinga and Ninety Mile Beach

Day trip 12 hrs

The top of the North Island is 210 km from Paihia — a full day on a coach tour that drives the sand of Ninety Mile Beach, sandboards the Te Paki dunes and reaches the lighthouse where the Tasman Sea and Pacific Ocean meet in a visible line of surf. Cape Reinga is where Māori spirits depart; the pōhutukawa on the headland is sacred. Our Cape Reinga guide has the detail.

12. Puketi kauri forest

Free 1 hr

Forty minutes west of Kerikeri, the Manginangina boardwalk is a 20-minute loop through standing kauri, some over 1,000 years old, in one of the larger surviving northern kauri forests. Clean your shoes at the stations — kauri dieback is spread by soil on boots. Guided night walks for kiwi run from Paihia.

13. Rainbow Falls and the Kerikeri River track

Free 1.5 hrs

A 27-metre fall on the Kerikeri River with a track from the Stone Store basin along the river (4 km, 1 hour each way) past the Wharepuke Falls and a fairy pool swimming hole. Or drive to the top car park for a five-minute view. The rainbow in the spray is a morning thing.

Best time to go: November to March

November to March is the bay at its best: sea temperatures reach 20–22°C, the pōhutukawa flower red along the coast in December, cruises and sails run daily, and the Cape Reinga tours have long enough days to do the full loop. January is busy — Paihia accommodation and the better cruises book out; November and March have the same weather with more space.

Bay of Islands questions

How many days do you need in the Bay of Islands?

Three days: one on the water (Hole in the Rock or a sail), one for Waitangi, Russell and Kerikeri, and one for the Cape Reinga day trip if you want it. Two days covers the first two.

Can you still swim with dolphins in the Bay of Islands?

Swimming with the resident bottlenose dolphins has been restricted by DOC since 2019 to protect a declining pod. Cruises still watch them from the boat and often spend 20–30 minutes with a pod; check the operator's current permit conditions.

How far is the Bay of Islands from Auckland?

About 230 km to Paihia, 3 to 3.5 hours by car on State Highway 1 via Whangārei. Buses run daily; there are no passenger trains. Kerikeri has a small airport with flights from Auckland.

What is the best time to visit the Bay of Islands?

November to March for warm sea (up to 22°C in February), settled sailing weather and the full cruise timetable. It is New Zealand's warmest region; late November and March avoid the Christmas–January peak.
