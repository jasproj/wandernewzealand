13 Best Things to Do in Wānaka, New Zealand

Wānaka is Queenstown's quieter neighbour an hour over the Crown Range — same mountains, same lake-and-alps setting, about a third of the people. It is the base for Mount Aspiring National Park, two of the South Island's most photographed walks, and a ski field in winter. This guide covers 13 things to do, with distances from town, how long each takes, and what to book ahead between November and March. Planning the trip? Walks and views

1. Roys Peak

Hike 5–6 hrs

The 16-km return climb to 1,578 metres is the most photographed track in New Zealand for a reason — the ridge viewpoint 30 minutes below the summit looks straight down Lake Wānaka to Mount Aspiring. It is relentless: 1,200 metres of gain on a farm track with no shade. Start at dawn in summer, carry two litres of water, and expect a queue at the lookout by 9 am. Closed 1 October to 10 November for lambing.

2. The Wānaka Tree

Free 10 min

A crack willow growing out of the lake 10 minutes' walk from town along the lakefront path toward Glendhu Bay. It is small, and it is still worth the walk at sunrise when the Southern Alps are lit and the water is flat. Afternoon light is from the wrong side. Do not climb it — a branch was cut by vandals in 2020.

3. Rob Roy Glacier Track

Hike 3–4 hrs

A 10-km return walk up the Rob Roy valley in Mount Aspiring National Park to a viewpoint under a hanging glacier, with waterfalls dropping off the ice. The track starts at Raspberry Creek, 54 km from Wānaka up the unsealed Matukituki Valley road with several fords — a guided trip handles the drive and the river crossings.

4. Mount Iron

Free 1.5 hrs

The short-walk alternative to Roys Peak: a 4.5-km loop up a glacier-carved hill on the edge of town for a 360-degree view of the lake, the Cardrona valley and the Pisa Range. Forty-five minutes up. Good for your arrival evening or a day when your legs are done.

5. Blue Pools and the Haast Pass road

Day trip 1 hr north

Sixty-five kilometres north on the road to the West Coast, a 30-minute return walk through beech forest reaches the Blue Pools, where the Makarora River runs glacier-clear and blue over gravel. The swing bridge was removed in 2024 pending replacement — check current access. Guided day tours combine it with the Bridle Track and Lake Hāwea lookouts.

On and over the lake

6. Lake cruise to Mou Waho island

Boat 3–4 hrs

Mou Waho is a predator-free island in the middle of Lake Wānaka with a lake on top of it — Arethusa Pool, 150 metres above the main lake. The cruise crosses from town, then a 30-minute guided climb reaches the pool with buff weka and views back down the lake. One of the better half-days here.

7. Jet boat the Clutha River

Adrenaline 30–60 min

The Clutha is New Zealand's highest-volume river and jet boats run from the outlet at Wānaka down through the braids and willow channels at speed. Shorter river runs suit families; the longer trips reach the Clutha's gorges. Morning departures have the stillest water.

Views 15 min aloft

Tandem parasailing from a boat on Lake Wānaka, towed to around 200 metres with the Southern Alps as the backdrop. Dry take-off and landing from the boat deck. Runs on calm days from the Roys Bay waterfront; book in the morning before the afternoon breeze.

9. Kayak or paddleboard from Roys Bay

Easy 1–2 hrs

The lakefront hire stands at Roys Bay rent single and double kayaks and paddleboards by the hour. Paddle west along the shore toward the Wānaka Tree and Ruby Island for flat water and a different angle on the mountains. The lake is cold year-round; summer afternoons bring a steady breeze.

Around town

10. Wānaka Lavender Farm

Family Dec–Feb bloom

Five minutes from town on the Cardrona road, a working lavender farm with rows in full purple from mid-December to late January, farm animals, a maze and a shop with the oils and honey. Entry is a few dollars. Pair with the afternoon's drive up the Cardrona valley.

11. Cardrona Hotel and the Crown Range Road

Scenic drive Historic pub

The 1863 Cardrona Hotel is one of the most photographed pubs in the country, 25 minutes from Wānaka on the road to Queenstown. Lunch in the garden, then carry on over the Crown Range summit (1,076 m, the highest sealed road in NZ) for the lookout above Arrowtown. Chains may be required in winter.

12. Puzzling World

Family 1–2 hrs

The leaning tower, the illusion rooms and a 1.5-km two-storey maze on the edge of town — a Wānaka fixture since 1973 and genuinely good on a wet day or with kids. Allow 90 minutes. The café is a reasonable lunch stop before Mount Iron, which starts across the road.

13. Cinema Paradiso and Rhyme & Reason

Evening Local

Cinema Paradiso screens films from sofas and an old Morris Minor, with fresh cookies at intermission; book ahead in summer. Rhyme & Reason brewery on Gordon Road runs the taproom locals use. Between them, that is a Wānaka evening.

Best time to go: November to March

November to March is walking season: Roys Peak reopens on 11 November after lambing, the Matukituki road to Rob Roy is normally fordable, and the lake is at its calmest in the mornings. Lavender peaks mid-December to late January. Daytime highs run 22–28°C in January; the lake stays cold.

Wānaka questions

How many days should you spend in Wānaka?

Two to three. One for Roys Peak or Rob Roy Glacier, one for the lake (Mou Waho cruise, jet boat or kayak), and a half day for Cardrona and the lavender farm. Add a day for a Blue Pools and Haast Pass run.

Is Wānaka better than Queenstown?

Different. Wānaka is quieter, cheaper for accommodation and closer to Mount Aspiring National Park; Queenstown has far more operators, nightlife and the Milford Sound day trips. Many people do both — they are an hour apart over the Crown Range.

When is Roys Peak closed?

The track closes annually from 1 October to 10 November for lambing. Outside that window it is open year-round; in winter it can carry snow and ice above 1,200 metres.

What is the best time to visit Wānaka?

November to March for walking, the lake and long days — the lavender blooms mid-December to late January. July to September is ski season at Cardrona and Treble Cone.
