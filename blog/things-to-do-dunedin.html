14 Best Things to Do in Dunedin, New Zealand

Dunedin is a Victorian university city built on Scottish money and gold, with a harbour that runs 20 km out to the Otago Peninsula — the only mainland breeding colony of royal albatross anywhere in the world. It gets a fraction of the visitors Queenstown does, which is part of the appeal. This guide covers 14 things to do in the city and on the peninsula, with how long each takes and which ones need booking. Planning the trip? Otago Peninsula wildlife

1. See royal albatross at Taiaroa Head

Wildlife Year-round

The Royal Albatross Centre at the tip of the peninsula overlooks the only mainland colony of northern royal albatross in the world — three-metre wingspans gliding over the headland. Birds are present year-round; chicks hatch in January and fledge around September. The guided tour includes the observatory; the Blue Penguins Pukekura evening viewing is at the same site.

2. Wildlife cruise to the albatross colony

Boat 1–2 hrs

The harbour cruise out past Taiaroa Head is the way to see albatross on the wing at sea level, with fur seals, shags and sometimes a sea lion on the way. Combination tours pair the cruise with a bus along the peninsula's high road and a stop at Larnach Castle. Afternoons tend to have more birds in the air.

3. Yellow-eyed penguins and sea lions at the beaches

Wildlife Dusk

Hoiho (yellow-eyed penguins) are the rarest penguin in the world and nest on the peninsula's ocean beaches. Guided tours use private hides at dusk; on public beaches like Sandfly Bay and Allans Beach you may see sea lions hauled out — keep 20 metres away, they are faster than they look. Numbers are low and declining, so a guided visit is more reliable.

4. Larnach Castle

Heritage 2 hrs

New Zealand's only castle, built 1871–87 by banker William Larnach on the peninsula's spine, with a ballroom, Georgian hanging staircase and seven hectares of gardens rated among the country's best. The tower gives views both sides of the peninsula. Twenty minutes from the city; tours run daily with high tea in the ballroom.

City

5. Dunedin Railway Station

Free Photo

The 1906 station in Flemish Renaissance style is reputedly the most photographed building in the country — Oamaru limestone and Central Otago bluestone outside, a mosaic floor of 750,000 Minton tiles inside. The Saturday farmers' market runs in the forecourt. Trains to the Taieri Gorge depart from here.

6. Ride the Taieri Gorge railway

Train 4 hrs return

The line climbs from the city into the Taieri Gorge over wrought-iron viaducts and through ten tunnels, built in the 1880s for the Central Otago goldfields. Dunedin Railways runs it to Pukerangi and back, with an open-air viewing carriage. Book ahead in summer; the one-way option connects to the Otago Central Rail Trail.

7. Speight's Brewery tour

Beer 90 min

Speight's has brewed on Rattray Street since 1876 and the tour walks the working brewery — the original gravity-fed brewhouse, the barley, the tap wall at the end with a self-pour session. The Ale House next door does the Southern Man menu. Book the late-afternoon slot and stay for dinner.

8. Baldwin Street

Free 15 min

The world's steepest residential street at a 1-in-2.86 gradient (about 19 degrees at the top), 3.5 km north of the centre. It is a 350-metre walk up; the view is modest but the gradient is not. Go early in the day before the tour buses, and park at the bottom.

9. Olveston House

Heritage 1 hr

A 35-room Edwardian mansion, left to the city with its contents intact in 1966 — the Theomin family's collection of art, Japanese ceramics, furniture and a 1921 Fiat still in the garage. Guided tours only, roughly hourly. Pairs with the nearby Otago Museum for a half day.

10. Street art trail and the Octagon

Free 1 hr

Dunedin's warehouse precinct south of the Octagon carries around 30 large murals by international artists, mapped on a free trail from the i-SITE. The Octagon itself is the city's eight-sided centre with the Dunedin Public Art Gallery (free), St Paul's Cathedral and the Robert Burns statue facing the pub, which locals consider correct.

11. Emerson's Brewery

Beer Taproom

Emerson's started craft brewing in Dunedin in 1992 and now runs a large taproom and restaurant on Anzac Avenue near the harbour, with tours of the brewhouse. If Speight's is the heritage pint, Emerson's is where Dunedin drinks now. Walkable from the station.

Day trips

12. Moeraki Boulders

Day trip 75 min north

Spherical concretions up to two metres across scattered along Koekohe Beach, 75 km north of the city on State Highway 1. Best at low tide and early morning. Combine with Fleur's Place at Moeraki village for lunch and the Katiki Point lighthouse for penguins and seals. Guided tours run from Dunedin that fold in Karitāne and local Māori history.

13. Tunnel Beach

Walk 1 hr return

A 20-minute descent through farmland to a sandstone headland on the city's south coast, with a hand-cut tunnel (1870s) dropping to a private-feeling cove. Sea arches and cliffs either side. The walk back up is steep. Closed for lambing in September–October some years — check before you go.

14. Surf or walk at St Clair

Beach Free

St Clair is the city's beach suburb, 10 minutes from the Octagon, with a reliable surf break, a heated outdoor salt-water pool on the rocks (October–March) and a row of cafés on the esplanade. The walk along the beach to St Kilda takes 30 minutes each way.

Best time to go: November to March

November to March. Albatross are at the colony all year but summer brings the courtship flights and, from late January, chicks on the nest. Penguin viewing runs year-round at dusk. The Taieri Gorge train and St Clair pool run full summer schedules. Expect 15–22°C and bring a windproof layer for the peninsula.

Dunedin questions

How many days do you need in Dunedin?

Two days: one for the Otago Peninsula (albatross, penguins, Larnach Castle) and one for the city and either the Taieri Gorge train or a Moeraki Boulders run. The peninsula day is the one not to cut.

Can you see penguins in Dunedin?

Yes. Yellow-eyed penguins nest on the Otago Peninsula's ocean beaches and little blue penguins come ashore nightly at Pilots Beach by the Royal Albatross Centre. Guided viewing at dusk is far more reliable than public beaches; numbers are small.

Is Larnach Castle worth it?

Yes if you like heritage houses and gardens. Allow two hours for the castle, tower and grounds; it is 20 minutes from the city on the peninsula high road, which is itself one of the better drives in the region.

What is the best time to visit Dunedin?

November to March for the longest days and mildest weather; albatross chicks hatch in late January and the St Clair hot salt-water pool is open. Dunedin is cooler than the north — pack for 15–22°C even in summer.
