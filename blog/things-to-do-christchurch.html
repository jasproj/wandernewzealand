14 Best Things to Do in Christchurch, New Zealand

Christchurch is the South Island's gateway city and most people treat it as a one-night stop before Kaikōura, Mount Cook or the West Coast. Give it two days and it earns them: the centre has been rebuilt around the Avon River since the 2011 earthquakes, the Port Hills sit ten minutes from downtown, and Akaroa on Banks Peninsula has the only place in the world you can swim with Hector's dolphins. This guide covers 14 things worth your time, grouped so you can pair a city day with a peninsula day. Planning the trip? City centre

1. Punt on the Avon River

Classic 30 min

Flat-bottomed punts leave from the Antigua Boat Sheds (built 1882) for a 30-minute glide through the Botanic Gardens, poled by a guide in Edwardian dress. It is unapologetically touristy and still the best way to see the river-and-garden heart of the city. Morning runs are quietest; book the last slot of the day in summer for low light on the willows.

2. Christchurch Botanic Gardens and Hagley Park

Free 2 hrs

Twenty-one hectares inside the loop of the Avon, with the rose garden peaking in November–December and the conservatory complex open year-round. Hagley Park wraps around it at 165 hectares, so you can run, bike or just cross it to reach the museum. The Saturday farmers' market at Riccarton House (10 minutes west) pairs well with a morning here.

3. Riverside Market and the rebuilt centre

Food Lunch

Riverside Market on Oxford Terrace is the city's indoor food hall — around 30 vendors from dumplings to Canterbury lamb, open daily. Walk out the back to the Bridge of Remembrance, the Cardboard Cathedral (the transitional cathedral built from 98 cardboard tubes in 2013) and the ChristChurch Cathedral reinstatement works in Cathedral Square, due to reopen later this decade.

4. Quake City and the Canterbury Museum

History 1–2 hrs

Quake City on Armagh Street is a compact, well-made exhibition on the 2010–11 earthquakes: the cathedral's fallen spire cross, survivor interviews, and the engineering behind the rebuild. The Canterbury Museum beside the Botanic Gardens is mid-redevelopment, with a smaller temporary venue on Hereford Street until the main building reopens around 2029.

5. Christchurch Gondola and the Port Hills

Views Half day

The gondola rises 500 metres from Heathcote Valley to a summit station with views over the city, Lyttelton Harbour's drowned volcano crater and across the plains to the Southern Alps. Walk down via the Bridle Path (1 hour) into Lyttelton for lunch at the Saturday market or Civil and Naval, then bus back through the tunnel.

6. Distillery and brewery tours

Evening Tasting

Christchurch has a strong small-batch scene. The Spirits Workshop in Woolston runs a one-hour tour of its gin and whisky stills with tastings, and the city's craft breweries — Cassels, Pomeroy's, Two Thumb — cluster within a short cab ride of the centre. Good for a wet afternoon, which Canterbury does produce even in summer.

Banks Peninsula and Akaroa

7. Swim with Hector's dolphins at Akaroa

Wildlife Nov–Mar

Akaroa Harbour is the only place in the world with permits to swim with Hector's dolphins, the world's smallest and rarest marine dolphin. Boats carry a capped number of swimmers, so book ahead. The dolphins come closer inshore November to March, which is also when water temperatures are survivable in the supplied wetsuits. Non-swimmers can take the nature cruise.

8. Sea-kayak Pōhatu Marine Reserve

Kayak Half day

A 4WD drive over the crater rim to Flea Bay, then a guided paddle along the cliffs of the Pōhatu Marine Reserve past kororā (little penguin) nesting burrows, fur seals and, in season, dolphins. Run by the family that farms the bay and runs the penguin conservation programme. Early-bird departures get the calmest water.

9. Evening penguin tour at Flea Bay

Wildlife Sep–Jan

Flea Bay holds the largest mainland colony of kororā (little penguins) in New Zealand, with nesting peaking September to January. The evening tour visits the nest boxes with a conservation guide before the birds come ashore at dusk. Numbers drop sharply after the moult in February, so this is a spring and early-summer thing.

10. Explore Akaroa village

French heritage Day trip

Akaroa was settled by French colonists in 1840, which survives in the street names (Rue Lavaud, Rue Jolie), the bakery and the cottages along the waterfront. It is 84 km from Christchurch — about 1 hr 20 min over the Summit Road, which is the drive to take. Allow a full day with a harbour cruise or kayak in the middle.

Beyond the city

11. Jet boat the Waimakariri braided river

Adrenaline Kaiapoi

Twenty minutes north of the city at Kaiapoi, jet boats run the shallow braids of the Waimakariri River — spins and shallow-water passes on a river that spreads a kilometre wide across the plains. It is the Canterbury version of the Shotover experience without the Queenstown price or queue.

12. Day trip to Arthur's Pass

Alps Full day

Two hours west on State Highway 73, Arthur's Pass National Park sits at the top of the Southern Alps. The Devils Punchbowl Falls walk (1 hour return) drops 131 metres beside the road, and kea are common in the village car park — keep an eye on your wiper blades. The TranzAlpine train covers the same route if you would rather not drive.

13. Riccarton House and Pūtaringamotu bush

Heritage 1 hr

The oldest house in Canterbury (1856) sits in the last remnant of kahikatea floodplain forest on the plains, a 7-hectare patch of trees up to 600 years old. Guided house tours run on set days; the bush loop is free and 15 minutes. The Saturday morning farmers' market in the grounds is the city's best.

14. New Brighton Pier and hot pools

Beach Sunset

The 300-metre concrete pier at New Brighton is the city's ocean-side outing, 20 minutes east of the centre. He Puna Taimoana, the hot salt-water pools beside the pier, open late into the evening and are the right end to a cold day. The beach runs for kilometres north toward Waimairi if you want a walk.

Best time to go: November to March

Plan Christchurch for November to March. The Akaroa dolphin swim is the anchor and it is at its best in summer water; Pōhatu penguin tours peak September–January; Arthur's Pass and the Summit Road are reliably open. Canterbury nor'westers can push the city past 30°C in January, and a cool southerly change can follow the same day — pack a layer.

Christchurch questions

How many days do you need in Christchurch?

Two days works: one for the city centre, Botanic Gardens, gondola and Lyttelton, and one for Akaroa and Banks Peninsula. Add a third if you want an Arthur's Pass or Kaikōura day trip rather than driving on.

Is Christchurch worth visiting after the earthquakes?

Yes. The centre was rebuilt almost from scratch between 2012 and 2020 around the Avon River, with Riverside Market, new laneways and the Tūranga library. Quake City explains what happened. Some gaps and construction remain around Cathedral Square.

How far is Akaroa from Christchurch?

About 84 km, 1 hour 20 minutes by car over the Summit Road, or 1 hour 30 by the shuttle bus. Dolphin swims and harbour cruises run from the Akaroa waterfront; book the swim ahead in summer because numbers per boat are capped.

What is the best time to visit Christchurch?

November to March. Summer gives long days, warm water for the Akaroa dolphin swim, the Botanic Gardens in flower and reliable mountain-pass access. Penguin tours at Flea Bay are best September to January while the birds are nesting.
