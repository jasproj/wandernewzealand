16 Best Things to Do in Rotorua, New Zealand

Rotorua is New Zealand's geothermal heartland — a city where mud pools bubble in public parks, steam rises from suburban drains, and the earth smells like sulfur. It is also the country's center of Maori culture and one of the North Island's best adventure towns. This guide covers the 16 best things to do, from geothermal parks and cultural experiences to mountain biking and natural hot springs.

Geothermal Experiences

1. Wai-O-Tapu Thermal Wonderland

Geothermal 2-3 hrs

The most colorful geothermal area in New Zealand. The Champagne Pool is a 65-meter-wide hot spring with vivid orange and green mineral deposits at its edges. The Artist's Palette is a flat expanse of pastel-colored silica, and the Devil's Bath is an almost neon-green pool. The Lady Knox Geyser erupts daily at 10:15 AM (induced with soap, but still impressive). Located 30 minutes south of Rotorua.

2. Te Puia — Pohutu Geyser and Maori Arts

Geothermal Culture

Home to Pohutu Geyser — New Zealand's largest active geyser, erupting up to 30 meters high several times daily. Te Puia also houses the New Zealand Maori Arts and Crafts Institute, where you can watch carvers and weavers at work. The Te Ra guided tour includes a cultural performance and a steam-box lunch cooked in geothermal vents. The evening Te Po experience adds a hangi feast.

3. Waimangu Volcanic Valley

Geothermal 2-3 hrs

The world's youngest geothermal system, created by the 1886 eruption of Mount Tarawera. A walking trail descends through a volcanic valley past Frying Pan Lake (the world's largest hot spring by surface area), Inferno Crater Lake (which cycles between bright blue and muddy every 38 days), and steaming cliffs. Less crowded and more natural-feeling than Wai-O-Tapu or Te Puia. An optional boat cruise on Lake Rotomahana adds volcano views.

4. Hell's Gate (Tikitere)

Geothermal Mud bath

The most active geothermal reserve in Rotorua with boiling mud pools, sulfur formations, and a hot waterfall — the only one in the Southern Hemisphere. What makes Hell's Gate different: you can finish with a genuine mud bath and sulfur spa. The mud is geothermal, not cosmetic — thick, warm, and genuinely therapeutic. George Bernard Shaw visited in 1934 and called it "hellish enough."

Maori Culture

5. Tamaki Maori Village

Evening 3.5 hrs

The most immersive Maori cultural experience in Rotorua. An evening visit includes a bus ride to a re-created pre-European village, a welcome ceremony with the haka, demonstrations of weaponry, games, and song, followed by a hangi feast (food cooked underground using geothermally heated stones). The experience runs 3.5 hours including transport. Book ahead — it sells out in summer.

6. Ohinemutu Maori Village

Free Living village

A living Maori village on the shore of Lake Rotorua where geothermal activity is woven into daily life — hot pools in backyards, steam rising from gardens, and cooking boxes over natural vents. St. Faith's Anglican Church has a unique window etching of Christ appearing to walk on Lake Rotorua. This is a real community, not a tourist attraction — visit respectfully and stick to public paths.

7. Te Puia Night Experience (Te Po)

Evening Culture

An alternative to Tamaki Village that combines geothermal viewing (Pohutu Geyser is dramatically lit at night) with a cultural performance and hangi dinner. The advantage: you see the geothermal area and get the cultural experience in one visit. The disadvantage: the cultural component is shorter than Tamaki's standalone evening.

Adventure Activities

8. Skyline Rotorua Luge

Family All ages

Gondola ride to the top of Mount Ngongotaha, then luge down on gravity-powered carts through three tracks — scenic (gentle), intermediate (banked corners), and advanced (steep drops and tight turns). Tickets include multiple rides. The views over Rotorua and the lake from the gondola are excellent. A zipline course operates from the same summit. Open year-round.

9. Zorbing (OGO)

Unique Rotorua original

Rolling downhill inside a giant inflatable ball was invented in Rotorua. OGO operates on a hillside north of town with three track options — straight, sidewinder (zigzag), and mega (the longest). You can go dry (bouncing) or wet (warm water inside the ball). It is one of those activities that sounds ridiculous and turns out to be genuinely fun. Suitable for ages 5 and up.

10. Whakarewarewa Forest Mountain Biking

MTB Free trails

One of the best mountain biking trail networks in the Southern Hemisphere — over 180 km of trails through redwood forest, from beginner-friendly gravel paths to technical single track. The trails are free to ride and well-maintained by the local cycling community. Bike rentals available from multiple shops on the forest edge. The same forest is home to the Redwoods Treewalk (see below).

11. White Water Rafting — Kaituna River

Grade 5 Highest commercially rafted waterfall

The Kaituna River has the highest commercially rafted waterfall in the world — the 7-meter Tutea Falls. The grade 5 section is intense but brief, and the rest of the river is grade 3-4 through a native bush gorge. No prior rafting experience is needed — guides brief you thoroughly. The Rangitaiki River is the mellower alternative (grade 3) if the waterfall sounds like too much.

Nature and Relaxation

12. Redwoods Treewalk

Forest Day or night

A series of 28 suspension bridges strung between century-old California redwood trees, 12 meters above the forest floor. The daytime walk is scenic; the nighttime version is lit with 30 lanterns designed by artist David Trubridge — a genuinely magical experience moving through the illuminated canopy. The nighttime walk is Rotorua's most Instagram-friendly activity. Book the last daytime slot for both experiences back to back.

13. Polynesian Spa

Hot springs Lakefront

Rotorua's premium hot spring complex on the shore of Lake Rotorua. Multiple pools at different temperatures fed by two natural springs — one acidic (good for skin), one alkaline (good for joints). The adult-only Lake Spa section has private rock pools overlooking the lake. Evening sessions are less crowded and more atmospheric. The Deluxe Lake Spa is worth the upgrade for the privacy and views.

14. Kerosene Creek

Free Natural hot spring

A free natural hot stream in the forest, 30 minutes south of Rotorua on the road to Wai-O-Tapu. The water flows through a bush-lined creek at a comfortable bathing temperature with a small waterfall. It is popular with locals and visitors alike — go early morning or late afternoon for fewer people. Bring your own towel, pack out your rubbish, and respect the site.

15. Lake Tarawera

Scenic Hot springs

A beautiful lake beneath Mount Tarawera (the volcano that erupted in 1886 and created the Waimangu Valley). Water taxi services run to Hot Water Beach — a lakeside beach where geothermal hot water seeps through the sand, creating natural hot pools at the water's edge. Swimming, kayaking, and fishing are all available. The lake is 15 minutes from central Rotorua.

16. Hobbiton Day Trip

Day trip 45 min drive

The preserved Lord of the Rings and Hobbit movie set in Matamata, 45 minutes from Rotorua. A guided tour walks through 44 hobbit holes, the Green Dragon Inn (where you get a complimentary drink), and the Party Tree. The gardens are maintained year-round with real plantings. Book direct or through a Rotorua-based tour operator for hotel pickup. See our road trip guide for combining Hobbiton with a wider itinerary.

Itinerary tip: The best Rotorua two-day plan — Day 1: Wai-O-Tapu in the morning (arrive when it opens for the smallest crowds), Kerosene Creek in the afternoon, Tamaki Maori Village in the evening. Day 2: Skyline Luge, Redwoods Treewalk (afternoon light is best), Polynesian Spa to finish.
