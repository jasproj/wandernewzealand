Dunedin Tours

The only mainland royal albatross colony in the world, yellow-eyed penguins on the Otago Peninsula, and a Victorian city built on gold.

About Dunedin

Dunedin is the South Island's wildlife capital. The Otago Peninsula, which curls east from the city, holds the Royal Albatross Centre at Taiaroa Head — the only place on a mainland anywhere that northern royal albatross breed — plus colonies of yellow-eyed penguins (hoiho), little blue penguins, New Zealand fur seals and sea lions. Half-day and full-day wildlife tours cover the peninsula by minibus with stops at private conservation reserves you cannot reach on your own.

On the water, wildlife cruises leave from the Otago Harbour and Wellers Rock and run out past the albatross cliffs to where the birds take off and land; from November the season's chicks are on the nests and adults are commuting constantly. Back in the city, the Edwardian railway station, Speight's brewery, the Baldwin Street climb and Larnach Castle fill the tour calendar.

Dunedin is also the jumping-off point for the Catlins coast to the south and the Central Otago rail trail inland.

Best time to go: November to March

Albatross activity at Taiaroa Head is highest from November through February, when the parents are feeding chicks and the wind is reliable enough for the big birds to fly. Yellow-eyed penguin chicks hatch in November and the adults come ashore every evening to feed them through January; evening tours are timed to that. Little blue penguins are viewable year-round at Pilots Beach.

Dunedin summer days are long (sunset after 9:30pm in December) and the weather is cooler than the north — bring a jacket for the peninsula even in January; the albatross cliffs are exposed.

What to know before you book

  • Otago Peninsula tours are 40–60 minutes from the city centre by minibus; most include pickup
  • Albatross chicks are on the nest from late January; flying adults are most active in summer wind
  • Yellow-eyed penguin viewing is from hides at dusk — evening tours run late in summer
  • Wildlife cruises depart from Wellers Rock and the city wharf; both pass Taiaroa Head
  • Sea lions haul out on Allans Beach and Sandfly Bay — keep 20 metres back
  • Cruise-ship days (Port Chalmers) fill tours — check the port schedule and book early

Dunedin questions

Where can I see penguins in Dunedin?

Yellow-eyed penguins at private reserves on the Otago Peninsula (guided tours only), little blue penguins at Pilots Beach beside the Royal Albatross Centre, and both from the water on peninsula wildlife cruises. Evening tours give the best yellow-eyed sightings as the birds come ashore.

Can you see albatross from a boat in Dunedin?

Yes. Harbour and coastal wildlife cruises run past the Taiaroa Head cliffs where the royal albatross nest; on a windy afternoon you will see adults landing and taking off. The land-based Royal Albatross Centre tour gets you inside the observatory.

How long should I spend on the Otago Peninsula?

A half-day covers the albatross centre or a wildlife cruise; a full day adds the yellow-eyed penguin reserves, Larnach Castle and the beaches. Most peninsula tours run 5 to 7 hours.

Is Dunedin a good base for the Catlins?

Yes. The Catlins coast (Nugget Point, Purakaunui Falls, Curio Bay) starts 90 minutes south and is a long day trip or a comfortable overnight loop back via Invercargill.
